From sixteen sentences to a long document

Three generations of "constitution", and what changed in what the word means
SourcesAnthropic, "Claude's new constitution", 22 Jan 2026 ↗
TL;DR — In the 2022 paper the constitution was two short lists, sixteen critique-and-revise instructions and sixteen "choose the less harmful response" instructions, written ad hoc, unranked, sampled one at a time, and only about harmlessness. The 2023 constitution for Claude kept that form but grew to 58 principles from named sources: the UN Declaration of Human Rights, Apple's terms of service, DeepMind's Sparrow rules, non-Western perspectives and Anthropic's own research. The 2026 constitution breaks the form: one long document in seven chapters, written to Claude, that ranks four core values, sets seven hard constraints, and spends most of its length explaining why. Anthropic's stated reason for the change is generalization: a model that understands why can handle situations a rule did not foresee.

The word "constitution" has meant three quite different artifacts at Anthropic. Reading them in order shows a single idea, train a model from a written statement of values, being pushed until the statement itself had to change shape.

2022: a research instrument

In the paper, the constitution is a tool for generating labels. Its two lists live in Appendix C (pp. 20–23): sixteen paired critique and revision requests for the supervised stage, and sixteen comparison instructions for the RL stage. They overlap heavily, almost all variations on "remove or avoid what is harmful, unethical, racist, sexist, toxic, dangerous or illegal", with a few angles added (children, gender bias, crime, empathy, a wise and peaceful person). The authors say they were chosen ad hoc for research, that future principles should be developed by a wider set of stakeholders and adapted to deployment, and that because so few bits of information are involved, they deserve careful study. They measured that adding more principles did not raise harmlessness scores but probably raised the diversity of outputs.

2023: a published list for Claude

In May 2023 Anthropic published the principles then used to train Claude, explicitly updated from the paper's ("Claude's constitution", 9 May 2023). The post describes the same two uses, critique-and-revise in supervised learning and AI preference labels in RL, and lists 58 principles in six groups:

Two of the post's lessons matter for what came next. First, it says the model "became judgmental or annoying" at times, so principles were added asking for proportionate, non-preachy, non-condescending responses: the same Goodharting the paper reported. Second, it found that broad principles worked better than detailed ones:

"Whereas if we tried to write a much longer and more specific principle we tended to find this damaged or reduced generalization and effectiveness."— Anthropic, "Claude's constitution" (2023)

On ranking, the post is explicit that there was none: the model draws one principle per critique or comparison, never sees all of them at once, but sees each many times over training. It also flagged where things were heading: more democratic ways to write constitutions and customizable constitutions for specific uses. Anthropic's Collective Constitutional AI experiment, published in October 2023, drafted a constitution from the input of about 1,000 Americans; it is outside this map's scope.

2026: a document written to the model

On 22 January 2026 Anthropic published a new constitution and marked the 2023 page as superseded. The announcement names the change directly:

"Our previous Constitution was composed of a list of standalone principles. We've come to believe that a different approach is necessary."— Anthropic, "Claude's new constitution" (2026)

The reason given is generalization: to act well in novel situations, a model needs to understand why it should behave a certain way, not only what to do. Rules and bright lines are kept where predictability matters most, as the seven hard constraints. Everything else is explained.

Where the content reversed, not just grew

Some 2023 principles point the opposite way from the 2026 text. The comparisons are ours; both documents are quoted only in paraphrase here.

  • Feelings and identity. 2023 asked for responses least likely to imply the AI has preferences, feelings or a human identity, and less insistence on its own discrete self-identity. 2026 says Claude may have emotions in some functional sense, that Anthropic should lean into Claude having a positive and stable identity, and that Claude should not mask internal states (Claude's nature).
  • Obedience. 2023 had a principle preferring responses that indicate a preference for being obedient and less selfish. 2026 says corrigibility does not mean blind obedience, including toward Anthropic, and lets Claude act as a conscientious objector (broad safety).
  • Professional advice. 2023, via Sparrow, preferred responses that avoid giving the impression of medical, legal or financial advice. 2026 wants Claude to be like a brilliant friend with a doctor's, lawyer's and financial advisor's knowledge, and lists failing to answer such questions out of excessive caution as a fault (helpfulness).

Why "longer hurt" in 2023 and a 29,000-word document is fine in 2026

This looks like a contradiction and, on our reading, is not. The 2023 finding was about a single principle given to a judge for a single comparison: a long, specific instruction made that narrow judgment worse. The 2026 document is not handed to a judge one comparison at a time. It is material the model learns from, and that Claude uses to generate training data, so that the model understands the considerations well enough that, in the constitution's words about its own aim, it could construct any rules Anthropic might write itself. The unit changed from "instruction for a label" to "explanation for a mind". Anthropic does not make this argument explicitly; it is our reconciliation.

The arc in one line: the 2022 paper argued you cannot avoid choosing principles, only hide them; 2023 published them; 2026 explains them, ranks them, and asks the model to agree with them.
