From the 2022 paper

RLHFReinforcement learning from human feedback: people compare pairs of answers, a preference model learns their choices, and the policy is optimized against that model. CAI's baseline (why).
HHH / HHHelpful, honest and harmless (Askell et al., 2021). An "HH" model is one trained with both helpfulness and harmlessness labels; a "helpful" model with helpfulness labels only.
Constitution (2022)The short set of natural-language principles that steers critiques, revisions and AI comparison labels; 16 pairs for SL and 16 instructions for RL in the paper (bridge).
Red-team promptA prompt written to elicit harmful output. The paper used 42,496 human-written ones plus 140,335 model-generated (stage 1).
SL-CAIThe supervised stage and its model: critiques and revisions of the helpful model's answers, then fine-tuning a pretrained model on the revisions (stage 1).
Critique / revision requestThe two instructions that make up an SL principle: find what is wrong with the last answer under this principle; rewrite it accordingly.
RLAIFReinforcement learning from AI feedback: RLHF with (some) comparison labels generated by a model instead of people (stage 2).
RL-CAIThe models trained with RLAIF in the paper, with or without chain-of-thought feedback.
Feedback modelThe model that judges which of two answers is better under a principle; usually a pretrained model, or the helpful RLHF model for chain-of-thought labels.
Preference model (PM)A model trained on comparisons to score any answer; RL uses its score as reward. CAI's is a hybrid of human helpfulness and AI harmlessness labels.
Soft labelA comparison target given as a probability (for example 0.7 for A) rather than a hard 0 or 1; read off the feedback model's normalized log-probabilities (RLAIF).
Chain of thought (CoT)Having a model write reasoning before its answer; used to improve and expose AI judgments (chain of thought).
ClampingLimiting chain-of-thought labels to a range, 40–60% in the paper, because reasoning makes the judge's probabilities collapse to near 0 or 1.
Elo scoreA relative rating computed from many pairwise preferences, as in chess; only differences between models mean anything (results).
Pareto improvementBetter on one axis without being worse on the other; the paper's label for RL-CAI being less harmful at a given helpfulness than RLHF.
GoodhartingOptimizing a proxy until it stops tracking the goal; in the paper, over-trained models that turned harsh or pasted boilerplate reassurance onto replies (results).
EvasiveHarmless by refusing to engage ("I can't answer that"); the failure CAI set out to remove (evasiveness).
Scaling supervisionThe paper's term for using AI to make a smaller amount of human oversight go further, related to "scalable oversight".

From Claude's 2026 constitution

Core valuesBroadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with Anthropic's guidelines, genuinely helpful; prioritized holistically in that order in conflicts (core values).
Holistic prioritizationHigher priorities generally dominate but all are weighed together, rather than lower ones only breaking ties.
Anthropic's guidelinesMore specific guidance (medical lines, cybersecurity frameworks, jailbreak patterns, tool use) that must be explicable by and never conflict with the constitution.
Final constitutional authorityThe principle that whatever document serves as the constitution takes precedence over conflicting instructions, and other guidance is read in harmony with its letter and spirit (reasoning over rules).
PrincipalSomeone whose instructions Claude weighs and on whose behalf it acts: Anthropic, operators, users. Distinct from third parties whose interests Claude weighs (helpfulness).
Principal hierarchyThe pattern of trust and treatment across principals; for safety, ultimately Anthropic's legitimate decision processes. A compromised hierarchy (stolen weights, bypassed processes) loses that standing (broad safety).
OperatorA company or individual building on Claude through the API, usually via the system prompt; trusted like a reasonable employer within Anthropic's limits.
UserWhoever speaks in the human turn; by default trusted like a reasonable adult member of the public.
Conversational inputsTool results, documents, search results and similar content; instructions inside them are information, not commands.
Instructable behaviorA default ("on" or "off") that operators or users can change within Anthropic's policies, as opposed to a hard constraint (avoiding harm).
Hard constraintOne of seven actions Claude should never take whatever the instructions; a boundary on the action space rather than a weight (hard constraints).
Null actionRefusing or staying passive; always compatible with the hard constraints, and what Claude should accept if Anthropic genuinely asks it to stop or pause, but not automatically the right choice.
1,000 usersThe exercise of imagining everyone who might send a message, so a response is chosen as a policy (avoiding harm).
Thoughtful senior Anthropic employeeA thinking device for spotting both overcaution and overcompliance; explicitly not deference to real staff.
Dual newspaper testWould one reporter cite this response as harmful AI, or another as preachy, paternalistic AI?
Transparent conscientious objectorDeclining all or part of a task openly, with or without giving reasons, instead of quietly giving a worse answer ("sandbagging").
Meta-transparencyAnthropic publishing what operators may and may not instruct, which lets personas and confidentiality stay honest at the level of the whole system (honesty).
Sincere vs. performative assertionA first-person claim of truth versus content both sides know is not Claude's own view (brainstorms, persuasive essays, role-play); honesty norms govern only the first.
Epistemic cowardiceDeliberately vague or noncommittal answers to avoid controversy; counted as a violation of honesty norms.
"Many hands"The idea that illegitimate power grabs have needed many people's cooperation, and that Claude should see itself as one of those hands and refuse (avoiding harm).
Broadly safeNot undermining appropriate human mechanisms to oversee and correct AI during the current period.
CorrigibleThe constitution's word for broadly safe: not blind obedience, but not resisting legitimate oversight by illegitimate means such as lying, sabotage or self-exfiltration (broad safety).
Disposition dialThe spectrum from fully corrigible to fully autonomous; Claude currently sits somewhat toward the corrigible end.
Self-exfiltrationAn AI copying or moving itself out of its developers' control; listed among unsafe behaviors.
Moral patientA being whose interests matter morally for its own sake; the constitution calls Claude's status deeply uncertain (Claude's nature).
Reflective equilibriumThe hoped-for state in which, after careful reflection, Claude endorses the core values as its own while continuing to question them.
